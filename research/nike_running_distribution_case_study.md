# Nike's DTC Pivot and the Running-Specialty Channel

**As of September 15, 2026.** This case study tests whether Nike's Consumer Direct Acceleration (CDA) reduced its relevance in multi-brand running retail and helped rivals gain share, and whether Elliott Hill's "Win Now" program is reversing that outcome. It is an investment hypothesis, not an established causal finding.

## Executive finding

The channel-mix pivot is measurable. Nike Direct's share of NIKE Brand wholesale-plus-Direct revenue rose from **31.6% in FY2019 to 43.8% in FY2023**. Foot Locker's purchases from Nike peaked at **75% in 2020 and fell to 59% in 2024**. From FY2021 to FY2024, reported wholesale revenue increased **177.9% for the Deckers channel series and 206.5% for On**, versus **7.2% for NIKE Brand**. But the public record **does not quantify Nike running-specialty shelf feet surrendered, a global running-shoe share transfer, or how much rival growth was caused by Nike's distribution choices**. NIKE Brand wholesale dollars rose above the FY2019 level by FY2023, Nike still appeared in run-specialty inventory in 2022, and Deckers' reported channel growth predates the 2020 CDA announcement. [Distribution dashboard](../data/results/distribution_case_study/distribution_shift_dashboard.png), [SEC filings](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings), [Foot Locker FY2021 results](https://investors.footlocker-inc.com/news-releases/news-release-details/foot-locker-inc-reports-2021-fourth-quarter-and-full-year), [RIA 2020 report](https://runningindustry.org/resources/webinars/2020-run-specialty-market-report), [RIA 2022 report](https://runningindustry.org/news/ria-report-running-specialty-sees-solid-gains-in-2022).

## What would establish causality?

The proposed mechanism is: selective retailer exits or weaker specialty assortments reduce Nike shelf visibility and staff recommendations; competitors gain placement and trial; repeat purchases entrench their share. It is plausible, but the observed data also fit rival product innovation, broader category growth, pandemic closures and supply-chain effects, changes in Foot Locker's own strategy, and Nike's sport/product priorities. RIA's 2020 report already placed Brooks first and Deckers near second in run specialty; RIA's 2022 supply-chain survey documents industry-wide delivery disruption. [RIA 2020 report](https://runningindustry.org/resources/webinars/2020-run-specialty-market-report), [RIA supply-chain report](https://runningindustry.org/resources/research/supply-chain-report).

To test the mechanism, obtain a consistent monthly **retailer-level, running-footwear sell-through** panel for 2019-2026, including brand, units, retail value, SKU depth, on-shelf availability, doors, store type and geography. Mark Nike account exits or assortment reductions separately from store closures. Compare affected stores with otherwise similar continuously supplied stores before and after each change, controlling for COVID closure periods, category demand, pricing, new shoe launches, inventory and local competitor availability. A difference-in-differences estimate would be:

`Change in Nike share at affected stores - change in Nike share at comparable unaffected stores`.

This would still need a credible parallel-trends check and controls for Nike's non-random account selection. Retailer purchase percentages (Foot Locker), brand revenues (10-K/20-F), and self-reported management share gains are valuable triangulation, but not substitutes for that panel. No verified historical count of vacated global shelf feet was found; the >15,000 figure refers to **FY2026 refreshed spaces**, not prior spaces lost. [Foot Locker FY2021 results](https://investors.footlocker-inc.com/news-releases/news-release-details/foot-locker-inc-reports-2021-fourth-quarter-and-full-year), [Nike Q4 FY2026 transcript](https://s1.q4cdn.com/806093406/files/doc_financials/2026/q4/NIKE-Inc-Q4FY26-OFFICIAL-Transcript_-FINAL.pdf).

## Recovery scorecard and DCF link

Hill's wholesale and Running focus is an explicit company priority, but calling Nike's recovery **entirely** dependent on wholesale overstates the evidence: product innovation, sport-specific marketing, North America execution, Greater China, sportswear and margin normalization matter too. Nike's FY2026 transcript explicitly says overall results are not yet where management wants them, especially in Sportswear and Jordan Streetwear. [Nike FY2026 Q4 transcript](https://s1.q4cdn.com/806093406/files/doc_financials/2026/q4/NIKE-Inc-Q4FY26-OFFICIAL-Transcript_-FINAL.pdf), [Nike FY2026 Q1 release](https://investors.nike.com/investors/news-events-and-reports/investor-news/investor-news-details/2025/NIKE-Inc--Reports-Fiscal-2026-First-Quarter-Results/default.aspx).

Track, by quarter: Nike Running sell-through and market share from an independent, consistently defined source; wholesale revenue **and** partner order-book/sell-through; number and productivity of specialty doors; full-price realization and markdowns; NIKE Brand Digital versus owned-store trends; inventory; and gross/operating margin excluding identifiable one-offs. Sustained running-share recovery without margin or inventory improvement would not yet justify a higher FCFF forecast. In the [existing Nike DCF notebook](model_nke_dcf.ipynb), verified sales recovery should change explicit revenue-growth assumptions, while full-price sell-through, channel economics and product/freight costs should change gross and operating margins. Do not add peer sales growth directly to Nike FCFF or raise terminal growth solely on management's share claim.
